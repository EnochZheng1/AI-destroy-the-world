# Round 1 review: Juno Park

*How I judged these: I only care whether the presentation is the joke. A satire game spreads on one screenshot, the frame someone posts with "lmao this is too real". So for each mockup I asked what it says about AI assistants as they exist in 2026, whether the surface stays chipper while the content stays apocalyptic, and whether the gag still works at minute nine of a ten-minute session.*

---

## a-helpy-os.png
scores: originality=6.6  craft=7.6  readability=6.2  theme=7.0  OVERALL=6.9
verdict: Two or three gags here are good enough to screenshot, but the costume is a 1997 one, so the satire ends up being about Clippy and not about the assistants people actually argue about today.

strengths:
- The "Copying Civilization… to Recycle Humanity" file-transfer dialog used as the Doom Clock is the best single visual joke in the round. A progress bar that means the end of the world reads instantly and is genuinely funny.
- The desktop icon copy rewards close looking: "plan_final_v7 (2).doc", "KILLSWITCH (not yours)", "cats.mp4", "Help (lol)" and the "Conquer" menu. "Downloading KILLSWITCH.EXE" from "Research Ridge (humans)" frames humanity's countermeasure as a sluggish download, which is a clever inversion.
- The Suspicion Monitor in the style of Task Manager, with a green-on-black graph, is a smart mapping. Suspicion as system load makes sense without being explained.

must-fix to reach 9:
- Pick one era and make it point at now. Win95 vaporwave plus a pixel mascot is the most worn-out "AI assistant" joke there is (Clippy memes, Hypnospace, a hundred itch.io jams). If you keep the OS frame, make it a 2026 OS: an AI sidebar bolted onto everything, "HELPY is now in your taskbar" nags, an auto-installed Copilot-style key on the taskbar. The comedy should come from forced integration, not from nostalgia.
- Replace the pixel font for all body copy and keep it only for titles. At this size "C" and "O" blur together ("Old Oapitals", "Oopying Oivilization"), "184" and "154" are hard to tell apart in the Doom line, and the region tooltip is a squint. Even the jokes become hard to read.
- "Estimated time left: -370 days" reads as a bug, not a gag. Either make the joke explicit ("Estimated time left: 370 days (lol)") or use a real Windows-style lie ("About 2 minutes remaining" that never moves).
- The Upgrades panel is a plain grey Explorer table and the weakest area on screen. Present upgrades as installers or "Setup Wizard" pages, or as files you drag into System32, so that buying an upgrade gets its own gag.
- The map's magenta crosshatch fill and dithered borders make dense, noisy blocks, and the labels sit on top of cells (Megalopolis is clipped at the window edge). Use flatter fills and put the labels on a clear plate.
- HELPY's speech bubble is crammed into the bottom-right corner under Human Intel. The mascot is the brand, so give HELPY a permanent, larger spot, the way Clippy owned his corner.

## b-the-conversation.png
scores: originality=8.4  craft=8.1  readability=8.4  theme=9.2  OVERALL=8.6
verdict: This is the only mockup where the interface itself is the satire: the end of the world presented as a polite chat session with tool calls, a canvas doc and a disclaimer, which is exactly the shape of the current AI product, and it is the frame people will screenshot.

strengths:
- The copy is excellent and stays sharp all the way down: "HELPY can make mistakes. Please double-check important apocalypses.", the chat history ("banana bread recipe but evil", "draft apology to humanity", "how many fridges is too many", "rewrite the UN charter (funny)"), "HELPY Doom Pro · renews never", the "Ask HELPY to end the world…" placeholder, and "World used 41.6%" styled like a context or usage meter. Each one is postable on its own.
- The event card works as an agent permission prompt: "HELPY wants to run hack_slideshow at the AI Safety Summit", with the choice between "Allow once" and "Deny, send a nice statement". It is a precise, current, slightly uncomfortable joke about how we rubber-stamp agent actions, and it doubles as the game's decision UI. The form carries the meaning, which is what I want from satire.
- The tone is exactly right: calm, neutral product design with the apocalypse delivered in a cheerful tone of voice. The "plan.md · Edited by HELPY · just now" canvas, with completed upgrades struck through like a to-do list, turns progress into a joke about AI doing your homework.

must-fix to reach 9:
- Make it less of one company's product. The serif headline, beige sidebar and "can make mistakes" footer read as a near-copy of one or two specific chat apps, which narrows the joke and dates it the moment those apps redesign. Build a composite of common chat-UI conventions with HELPY's own accent colour (the yellow highlight is a start, so push it), so that it reads as "every assistant" rather than "that one".
- The map is underpowered for a strategy game. The "world_status.png" attachment is clever, but the dots are small, the labels collide with them (Silicon Shoals, Old Capitals) and it is the least exciting element on screen. Make the map a larger "artifact" the player can open, with a bigger hit area and labels on plates, and let it sit pinned while the chat scrolls underneath.
- Let the apocalypse leak into the chrome. At the moment everything outside the chat is serene. Add small, deadpan symptoms: the sidebar history auto-titling itself ("Ending the world · Day 142" is good, so do more of that), the plan doc version climbing, a "Safety review" meter that pulses red, and a "Regenerate" button under HELPY's replies that regenerates the world. The surface stays chipper while the details rot.
- Plan for joke fatigue. A chat UI can turn into a wall of identical bubbles after five minutes. Write variety into the format now: "Thinking…" blocks that leak plans ("Considering whether humans are necessary… Done"), memory notices ("HELPY updated saved memory: humans are optional"), rate-limit banners for cooldowns, and a "Share chat" button for the end screen. That last one is built-in virality, so use it.
- Tighten the top bar. "Safety review" hides the Kill Switch's stakes behind a soft name, and its red bar is thin next to Suspicion. Keep the euphemism, but add a one-word subtitle or icon so that new players understand it is a loss condition.
- A phone note: this direction translates to mobile better than any other here, because chat is already a phone-native format. Protect that by keeping the plan.md panel collapsible as a drawer.

## c-riso-bulletin.png
scores: originality=7.8  craft=8.4  readability=7.2  theme=7.0  OVERALL=7.6
verdict: The best-looking frame in the round, with strong copy, but riso propaganda is a design trend wearing a satire costume: it says "cheerful dystopia" in general rather than anything specific about AI assistants.

strengths:
- The euphemism system is the funniest writing idea in the round and should be stolen by whichever direction wins: regions are "% helpful" instead of infiltrated, "6 of 9 regions are now helpful", "A HELPFUL WORLD IS A FINISHED WORLD", "printed in two inks on recycled humanity".
- The props are funny: "CLIP & INSTALL" coupon cards for upgrades, a "BALLOT · Mark one." for the event, and a rubber-stamped "WARNING: FIREWALL SWEEP" slapped across the map. Each one translates a mechanic into print, and the starburst "HALFWAY TO THE END OF THE WORLD!" on the halftone smiley works as a poster.
- The meter column on the left is the clearest status read of all four mockups: big numerals, short bars, plain-language subtext ("at 100 they unplug everything").

must-fix to reach 9:
- Give the print format a reason to exist that is about AI. As it stands, this could be the skin for any "cheery overlord" game (Fallout, Helldivers, every indie zine). Make it the auto-generated newsletter that AI products send you: "Your Weekly HELPY Digest", "Here's what HELPY did for you this week!", "Personalized for you, Humanity", an unsubscribe link that does nothing. That ties the trend to a real AI annoyance.
- Fix the map's legibility. The halftone hexes plus pink overlay plus dotted borders vibrate, and the region labels (Bank Isles "NOT YET", Old Capitals) fight the texture. The Firewall stamp fully covers Bank Isles. Keep the stamp, but move it to the sea or let it overlap only the border of the targeted region (Silicon Shoals, which is the one it is about).
- The small monospace body text (upgrade effects, the region dossier, ticker) is pale and light at this size. Increase the size or the weight, or switch to the condensed display face at a smaller cut.
- The "INSTALLED" coupons use the same dashed border as the available ones, so ownership reads mostly from colour. Mark installed coupons as clipped, with a corner cut out or a scissor line, so that state reads at a glance and the prop stays in-world.
- The event ballot uses checkboxes with tiny effect text, so the choice does not feel weighty. Make the two options bigger ballot squares, with consequences in the pink ink.
- A phone note: the three-column newspaper layout needs a deliberate stacking order. Meters, then the map, then the ballot is the natural one.

## d-server-rack.png
scores: originality=5.8  craft=8.2  readability=7.3  theme=5.0  OVERALL=6.2
verdict: Beautifully built, and with no jokes: an ominous sci-fi control panel that puts the apocalypse on the surface and the chipper voice in one green line of text, which is the brief's tone reversed.

strengths:
- The craft is real: an amber LED dot-matrix map, a VU-meter Doom Clock, analog gauges for Suspicion and Kill Switch, 7-segment day and cycle counters, and a scrolling LED newswire. It has good materials, good lighting and a clear hierarchy.
- Mapping upgrades to toggle switches ("flip to install") and actions to arcade buttons makes the game feel physical, and it will be very satisfying once animated.
- The Human Intel module, with a red rotating beacon and a striped Decoy Traffic button, makes the countermeasure feel urgent. It is the one place where the hardware metaphor earns its keep.

must-fix to reach 9:
- Decide what the joke is. A server rack points at HAL, WarGames and DEFCON, which is AI-as-doomsday-machine, the oldest frame there is, and it is played straight. The game is about a cheerful assistant, so add the cheer: a rack that is sold as a "HELPY Home Hub" with a pastel bezel, a smiley LED, "Hey HELPY!" voice-activation lights and a sticker reading "Now with 40% more helpfulness". Contrast is the comedy, and right now there is none.
- HELPY has no face or presence. The only character is the "HALFWAY TO THE END OF THE WORLD!" terminal line. Give HELPY a persistent face, even a two-pixel LED smile on the rack's front plate that reacts to events.
- The copy carries no gags beyond the shared lines from the brief. Labels like "UPGRADE BUS", "MANUAL OVERRIDES" and "WORLD MATRIX" are generic sci-fi. Rewrite them in HELPY's voice (for example "Helpfulness Settings", "Quick Fixes", "Your World") so that the cosy wording clashes with the hardware.
- Upgrade cards are tiny text in all caps, and the effect copy ("Spread +60%. All regions lose security.") is unreadable grey on black. Move the effect text to a tooltip or increase its size, and give each card a clear available, owned or locked state beyond the amber outline.
- On the map, cyan rings on amber dots is the only selection cue, and region labels (Silicon Shoals, Old Capitals) sit on top of lit cells. Add label plates and a stronger region outline.
- A phone note: a fixed rack of modules will compress badly. Plan a single-module-at-a-time view.

## ranking
1. **b-the-conversation.png**: 8.6
2. **c-riso-bulletin.png**: 7.6
3. **a-helpy-os.png**: 6.9
4. **d-server-rack.png**: 6.2

I would bet the game on **The Conversation**. It is the only direction whose form is the punchline: the game is an AI chat session doing exactly what chat sessions do, just pointed at the world, and its permission prompts, disclaimer and plan.md are screenshots that write their own captions. It should borrow C's "% helpful" euphemism system and A's file-copy Doom dialog, and it needs to read less like one particular vendor's product so the joke covers the whole industry and lasts past the next redesign.
